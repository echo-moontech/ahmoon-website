Decamethylcyclopentasiloxane (D5) Technical Guide: CAS 541-02-6

The volatile carrier that leaves nothing behind — specification table against TDS, a real 10-tonne batch COA, the four industries where D5 earns its keep, and the regulatory picture every formulator should know before ordering.

📅 Published 2026-11-11 ⏱ 8 min read 📂 Cyclosiloxane ✅ Verified against batch COA

Most silicones are prized for staying put — sealants, greases, potting compounds. Decamethylcyclopentasiloxane (D5, CAS 541-02-6) is prized for the opposite: it is a cyclic siloxane engineered to evaporate completely without residue, carrying actives, silicone oils or release chemistry onto a surface and then disappearing. That single property explains why D5 shows up in antiperspirant sticks, shoe-sole demolding, soft-touch textile finishes and precision cutting fluids alike.

This guide covers our ZJ-D5 grade end to end: the specification table, a real batch COA from a 10,000 kg production lot, dosage guidance per application, packaging and transport, and the compliance context (EU cosmetics restriction, D4 content) that professional buyers ask about first.

1. What D5 Is and How It Behaves

D5 is a five-membered ring of alternating silicon and oxygen atoms, each silicon bearing two methyl groups. The ring structure is the key:

  • Volatile without residue — evaporates cleanly at skin or process temperature, leaving no oily film; this is the "transient carrier" role that linear PDMS cannot play;
  • Excellent spreading and a soft, non-greasy feel — filmed on skin it gives a comfortable, warm sensation, the reason it dominates antiperspirant and skin-care formulas where permitted;
  • Strong solvent power — dissolves dimethyl silicone oil, ethanol and many organic solvents/miscible systems, making it a universal carrier for actives and silicone fluids;
  • Low viscosity (4.5–6.5 mPa·s) — wets molds, fibers and metal surfaces instantly;
  • Colorless, transparent, odorless, non-toxic and non-irritating per our TDS.

Why two flash-point figures? Closed-cup methods trap the vapor before ignition, reading 60–70 °C; the Cleveland open cup lets vapor disperse, reading 90–100 °C. Both describe the same liquid — always compare like with like when qualifying suppliers. In handling terms, D5 is combustible: keep containers closed, bond and ground during transfer, and stay away from heat, sparks and flame.

3. Where D5 Is Used — and at What Dosage

Our TDS puts general dosage between 5% and 100% depending on the formulation role. The four biggest application fields:

3.1 Personal care and cosmetics

The flagship market. D5 serves as a volatile emollient and carrier in antiperspirants and deodorants, hair sprays, facial cleansers, skin creams and lotions, bath oils, sunscreens, shaving creams and nail polish. It spreads actives evenly, then evaporates — delivering the dry-touch finish consumers expect. Formulators specify high-purity, low-D4 material; that is exactly the grade we ship (see the COA below).

3.2 Mold release agent

D5 carries silicone release chemistry into every detail of a mold, then flashes off — leaving a thin, uniform release film without build-up. Typical uses: shoe-sole (EVA/PU/rubber) molding, rubber and plastic parts, die-casting auxiliary release. The carrier volatility prevents residue accumulation that would eventually mark the cast part.

3.3 Textile softener and water repellent

In textile finishing, D5 is the standard carrier medium for silicone softeners (amino and hydroxyl silicones) and is itself an intermediate in water-repellent treatments. It distributes the active silicone evenly across the fiber, then evaporates — no yellowing, no sticky residue, reproducible hand feel batch after batch.

3.4 High-grade lubricants and cutting fluids

D5 adjusts the viscosity and volatility profile of precision lubricants and metal-working cutting fluids, and serves as a solvent carrier for silicone-oil-based lubricant blends. Its low surface tension improves wetting of machined surfaces.

5. The Regulatory Picture (What Buyers Ask First)

Cyclic siloxanes get regulatory attention, so here is the honest context:

  • EU cosmetics: D4 and D5 are restricted in wash-off cosmetic products to ≤0.1% since 2020 (Regulation (EU) 2018/35 amending 1223/2009). Leave-on products are not currently restricted. Verify your target market and product category before finalizing a formula — our TDS/MSDS package supports compliance review.
  • Toxicology: the rat inhalation study cited in our MSDS showed effects only at the highest exposure dose (160 ppm), a level that greatly exceeds typical workplace or consumer exposure; industry assessments conclude normal use does not represent a risk to humans.
  • Environment: D5 is volatile and has a very short half-life in aquatic environments; it is efficiently removed (>90%) during wastewater treatment and does not present a risk to aquatic organisms per the MSDS environmental assessment.
  • Transport: per our MSDS Section 14, D5 is not restricted for road/rail and ocean shipment is not subject to IMDG code — though its combustibility still calls for standard chemical-handling precautions (avoid fire, keep containers closed).

6. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is decamethylcyclopentasiloxane (D5) used for?
D5 is a volatile cyclic siloxane used as a carrier fluid and transient base in personal care (antiperspirants, hair sprays, cleansers, creams, bath oils, sunscreens, shaving foams, nail polish at 5–100% dosage), as a solvent carrier for dimethyl silicone oil and other organics, in mold release agents for shoe-sole and rubber molding, as a textile softener and water-repellent intermediate, and in high-grade lubricants and cutting fluids.
Q: What is the difference between D4 and D5?
D4 (octamethylcyclotetrasiloxane, CAS 556-67-2) has four Si-O units in the ring and is mainly a chain-building intermediate for polymer production. D5 (CAS 541-02-6) has five units, which makes it heavier and less volatile than D4 but still evaporable without residue — ideal as a carrier in personal care. High-purity D5 (99.97% in our batch COA) with D4 kept below 0.1% is exactly what cosmetic and formulation customers specify.
Q: Why does the D4 content matter when buying D5?
Regulatory attention on cyclic siloxanes focuses on D4, and cosmetic regulations limit both D4 and D5. Our batch COA shows D4 at 0.0029% (29 ppm) — three decades below the 0.1% limit — with total other cyclics at 0.0072%. That gives formulators a wide compliance margin and consistent volatility behavior batch after batch.
Q: What is the flash point of D5 silicone fluid?
It depends on the test method: closed-cup testing gives roughly 60–70 °C, while the Cleveland open-cup method (as in our MSDS) reads 90–100 °C because the open cup allows vapor to disperse. In practice, treat D5 as a combustible liquid: keep containers closed, bond and ground drums during transfer, and keep away from heat, sparks and flame.
Q: Is D5 restricted in cosmetics?
The EU Cosmetics Regulation restricts D4 and D5 in wash-off cosmetic products to 0.1% or less (since 2020); leave-on products are not currently restricted. Canada and other markets have their own rules. Because D5 evaporates without residue, many formulators still use it where allowed or choose alternatives for wash-off EU products — always verify your target market's current regulation. Our TDS and MSDS are available to support your compliance review.
