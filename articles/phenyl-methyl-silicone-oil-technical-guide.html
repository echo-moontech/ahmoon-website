Methyl Phenyl Silicone Oil Technical Guide: Grades 250/255/274/275

What one phenyl group does to a silicone fluid — from -70 °C lubrication to ultra-high vacuum at 10-8 Pa — with real specification tables, a full Dow Corning / Shin-Etsu cross-reference and batch COA data for every grade we ship.

📅 Published 2026-11-11 ⏱ 9 min read 📂 Silicone Oil ✅ Verified against batch COA

Regular dimethyl silicone oil covers most jobs — but some applications push a fluid further than a plain PDMS chain can go: -45 °C cold starts, 300 °C bearing greases, gamma radiation inside nuclear plants, or vacuums down to 10-8 Pa. That is where methyl phenyl silicone oil (CAS 63148-58-3) earns its place: introducing phenyl groups into the siloxane backbone lowers the freezing point, raises flash point and radiation stability, and pushes vapor pressure far below anything a dimethyl fluid can reach.

We manufacture three families of phenyl silicone oil — 250-30 (low-viscosity general purpose), the 255 series (multi-purpose, five viscosity steps) and the 274 / 275 ultra-high vacuum diffusion pump oils. This guide walks through their actual specification tables, a batch COA, and a cross-reference table to the Dow Corning and Shin-Etsu equivalents your procurement team already knows.

1. Why the Phenyl Group Changes Everything

On a PDMS backbone, a fraction of the methyl side groups is replaced by phenyl (C6H5–) groups. The bulky, polarizable phenyl rings deliver five practical consequences:

  • Lower freezing point — phenyl groups disrupt crystallization, so 250-30 stays pourable down to -45 °C (dimethyl oils of similar viscosity gel earlier);
  • Wider temperature window — flash points from 240 °C up to 320 °C with heat loss under 2% after 2 h at 250 °C, supporting service up to ~300 °C;
  • Radiation resistance — phenyl groups dissipate radiation energy, which is why these oils serve as heat carriers in nuclear plant applications;
  • Low vapor pressure — 275 grade saturates below 6.65 × 10-9 Pa, the key metric for diffusion pump oil;
  • High refractive index (1.47 up to 1.5785) — useful for optical coupling and as a formulation base.

6. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What does the phenyl group add to silicone oil performance?
Introducing phenyl groups into the siloxane chain raises the refractive index, lowers the freezing point, improves oxidation and radiation stability, and suppresses vapor pressure. Practically, methyl phenyl silicone oil stays fluid at -40 to -45 °C, tolerates continuous service up to about 300 °C, withstands gamma radiation (nuclear plant heat carrier duty), and reaches vapor pressures as low as 6.65 × 10-9 Pa for vacuum pump grades.
Q: How do I choose between grades 250-30, 255, 274 and 275?
250-30 (25–40 mm²/s) is a low-viscosity general-purpose fluid for impregnation, damping and cosmetic formulations. The 255 series (50–1,000 mm²/s) covers high-temperature grease base oil, heat transfer fluids and lubrication from -70 to 300 °C. 274 and 275 are dedicated ultra-high vacuum diffusion pump oils: 274 (38 mm²/s, ~60% phenyl content) works at 175–210 °C, while 275 (165–185 mm²/s, ~98% content) works at 180–230 °C and reaches lower ultimate pressure.
Q: Can your phenyl silicone oil replace Dow Corning DC-510/550/704/705 or Shin-Etsu KF-50/KF-54?
Yes — that is exactly how these grades are engineered. 250-30 corresponds to DC-510 / KF-50, the 255 series to DC-550, DC-710 / KF-54, 274 to DC-704 / Hivac F-4, and 275 to DC-705 / Hivac F-5. Customers routinely switch during shortages or price pressure; we recommend a 1–2 L trial run to confirm viscosity and vacuum performance in your equipment.
Q: What is the difference between diffusion pump oil 274 and 275?
274 is a lower-viscosity oil (38 ± 2 mm²/s, refractive index 1.5530–1.5590, freezing point about -35 °C) with about 60% phenylated siloxane content, operating at 175–210 °C. 275 is heavier (165–185 mm²/s, refractive index 1.5765–1.5785, freezing point -14 to -18 °C) with about 98% content, operating at 180–230 °C and delivering a lower ultimate vacuum (< 6.65 × 10-8 Pa vs < 3.99 × 10-7 Pa). Choose 275 when the lowest pressure matters; 274 for faster warm-up and general vacuum metallurgy.
Q: Is methyl phenyl silicone oil classified as a dangerous good?
No. Per GB 3000-2013 classification the material is not classified as dangerous goods: no hazard pictogram, no warning words and no transport restrictions. The composition is phenylated siloxane (CAS 63148-58-3) at ≥95%. Standard chemical logistics apply, and both sea and air freight are acceptable.

Standard packaging is 5 kg / 20 kg plastic pails and 200 kg drums; IBC totes are available for the 255 series. Free 100–500 g samples are provided for lab evaluation — you only cover the courier cost.
