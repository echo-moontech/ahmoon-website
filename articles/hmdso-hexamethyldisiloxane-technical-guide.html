Hexamethyldisiloxane (HMDSO, CAS 107-46-0): End-Capping, Hydrophobic Treatment & Plasma Coating Guide

The shortest siloxane chain — MM. A volatile, residue-free trimethylsilyl donor that caps silicone polymers, renders fillers water-repellent and builds plasma coatings — without corrosive by-products.

📅 October 28, 2026 📂 Silicone Rubber · Coatings · Pharma · Electronics ⏱️ 8 min read

What HMDSO Is and How It Works

Hexamethyldisiloxane (HMDSO), often abbreviated MM in silicone nomenclature, is the simplest member of the siloxane family: two trimethylsilyl groups joined by a single oxygen bridge, (CH₃)₃Si–O–Si(CH₃)₃. It is produced industrially by the controlled dechlorination–condensation of trimethylchlorosilane (TMCS) with water.

What makes it useful is what it lacks: there is no Si–H, no Si–OH and no Si–Cl in the molecule. It is chemically neutral, highly volatile (boiling point 101 °C) and leaves no residue after evaporation. At the same time, the Si–O bonds are polarizable, so HMDSO readily delivers trimethylsilyl groups to active sites — capping silanol groups (Si–OH) on polymer chains, pigment and filler surfaces with a thin, extremely hydrophobic trimethylsilyl layer.

That combination — a clean, volatile trimethylsilyl donor with no corrosive by-products — explains why HMDSO shows up everywhere from silicone rubber plants to plasma coating reactors and pharmaceutical synthesis.

HMDSO vs HMDS vs TMCS: Which Trimethylsilyl Donor?

HMDSO is one of three common trimethylsilyl donors, and buyers frequently mix them up. They are not interchangeable — the by-product each one releases determines where it can be used:

Rule of thumb: if your process cannot tolerate corrosive or basic by-products — silicone polymer finishing, powder treatment, plasma work, precision cleaning — HMDSO is usually the right choice. If you need a stronger silylation reagent for drug intermediates or wafer priming, look at HMDS instead.

Typical Applications

🔗 Silicone End-Capping (Blocking Agent)

Caps the terminal silanol groups of hydroxyl-terminated PDMS in 107 rubber and silicone fluid production, precisely controlling molecular weight. Because excess HMDSO evaporates completely, the finished polymer contains no residual capping-agent contamination.

🧪 Hydrophobic Powder & Filler Treatment

Treats fumed and precipitated silica, diatomite, talc and kaolin: surface Si–OH groups are capped with trimethylsilyl groups, converting hydrophilic powders into free-flowing, water-repellent grades for coatings, sealants and rubber compounds.

💡 Plasma Polymerization (PECVD) Precursor

HMDSO vapour polymerizes under low-pressure plasma into SiOCH organosilicon films; with an O₂ feed, the film shifts toward hard silica-like glassy coatings. Used for anti-scratch optical coatings, hydrophobic glass, barrier layers on polymers and biomedical surface modification.

🧴 Release & Cleaning Agent

Works as a mould release agent in rubber and plastics processing and as a precision cleaning / degreasing solvent in electronics, where complete evaporation without residue is essential.

💊 Pharmaceutical & Analytical

Used in pharmaceutical synthesis, as a gas-chromatography stationary phase (silicone type) and as an analytical reagent in laboratory practice.

🏭 Silazane Raw Material

Serves as a feedstock for producing hexamethyldisilazane (HMDS) and polysilazane resins — the route to ceramic-forming coatings such as 9H hard coats.

How to Use It

The right process depends on the application, but three patterns cover most uses:

  1. End-capping of silicone polymers — dose HMDSO against the measured hydroxyl (Si–OH) content of the polymer: the capping ratio of MM to Si–OH determines the final molecular weight. Reaction proceeds with an acid or alkaline (e.g. KOH) catalyst at elevated temperature; afterwards, unreacted MM and volatile by-products are stripped off, and because HMDSO is fully volatile the stripping step leaves no residue in the polymer.
  2. Filler / powder surface treatment — apply HMDSO neat (vapour-phase or direct dosing in a mixer) or as a solution onto the siliceous filler, then heat at 100–120 °C to complete the silylation reaction and remove volatiles. Dosage is governed by filler surface area; start with a small trial treating three loading levels to find the minimum dose that reaches the target hydrophobicity (methanol-wettable → water-repellent transition).
  3. Plasma deposition — feed HMDSO vapour with a carrier gas (Ar, N₂ or He) into a low-pressure RF plasma. A pure HMDSO plasma yields soft, polymer-like SiOCH films; adding O₂ increases in-film oxygen and produces harder, more silica-like, transparent coatings. Film properties are tuned through monomer flow, power and pressure — we can share starting parameter windows for your reactor geometry on request.

Safety note: HMDSO is a Class 3 flammable liquid with a flash point of 0 °C. Ground and bond containers, use explosion-proof equipment and non-sparking tools, and prevent static discharge during transfer. Avoid release to the environment — it is classified as toxic to aquatic life.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is HMDSO used for?
HMDSO is mainly used as an end-capping (blocking) agent for hydroxyl-terminated silicone polymers, as a hydrophobic surface treatment for silica, diatomite, talc and other siliceous fillers, as a cleaning and release agent, as a raw material for silazane production, and as a precursor for plasma polymerization (PECVD) coatings. It is also used in pharmaceutical synthesis, GC stationary phases and water-repellent treatments.
Q: What is the difference between HMDSO and HMDS?
HMDSO is hexamethyldisiloxane (CAS 107-46-0) — two trimethylsilyl groups bridged by oxygen. HMDS is hexamethyldisilazane (CAS 999-97-3) — the same groups bridged by nitrogen. HMDS releases ammonia and is the stronger silylation reagent (photoresist priming, pharma synthesis); HMDSO is neutral, volatile and residue-free, preferred for end-capping, cleaning, release and plasma work. TMCS (CAS 75-77-4) is the most reactive of the three but releases corrosive HCl.
Q: Is HMDSO a dangerous good?
Yes — Class 3 flammable liquid (H225), flash point 0°C. It must be transported and stored under flammable-liquid regulations, away from ignition sources, using grounded and bonded equipment. It is also toxic to aquatic life, so environmental release must be avoided. We ship worldwide in compliant DG packaging with full documentation.
