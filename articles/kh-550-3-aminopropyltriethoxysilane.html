KH-550 Amino Silane (3-Aminopropyltriethoxysilane): A Chemical Bridge Between Inorganic Surfaces and Organic Resins

Coatings and composites rarely fail in the bulk. They fail at the interface — the boundary where an organic polymer meets an inorganic surface.

📅 September 11, 2026 📂 Thermoset Composites · Adhesives · Coatings ⏱️ 8 min read

Why Failure Usually Starts at the Interface

Concrete, glass, silica sand, quartz and most mineral fillers are hydrophilic and chemically inert toward organic resins. An epoxy, phenolic or polyester system will cure into a hard film on top of them — but it never forms a real chemical bond with the surface.

The consequences show up later, usually in this order:

  • Adhesion loss and edge lift after moisture exposure
  • Fillers and pigments that agglomerate, settle, or debond from the matrix
  • Reinforced plastics that fall short on bending, compressive and shear strength
  • Molding sand that loses strength and moisture resistance in service
  • Premature failure at the substrate while the coating film itself is still intact

The instinct is to reformulate the resin. But no resin reformulation fixes a substrate that chemically refuses to bond.

What KH-550 Does

MOON-KH550 is 3-aminopropyltriethoxysilane. The molecule carries two different reactive ends:

  • An ethoxy end that hydrolyses to a silanol and condenses with hydroxyl groups on the inorganic surface, forming a stable Si–O–Si / Si–O–M linkage.
  • An amino end that reacts into epoxy, phenolic, polyester, polyurethane, PBT, polyamide and polycarbonate systems.

Where the two ends meet, the interface stops being a boundary and becomes part of the structure. Formulators specify KH-550 for:

  • Higher dry and wet adhesion between polymer and filler
  • Better wettability and dispersion of fillers and pigments in the polymer
  • Improved bending, compressive and shear strength in reinforced systems
  • Better retention of electrical and mechanical performance under humid conditions
  • Improved water resistance and aging resistance of the finished product

Bond promoter for polyurethane, epoxy, nitrile and phenolic adhesives. Improves adhesion to glass, aluminum and iron.

🎨 Coatings

Used in polyurethane, epoxy and acrylic latex coatings to improve substrate adhesion and pigment dispersion.

How to Use It

KH-550 is typically applied in one of three ways:

  1. Dry-blended onto the filler or pigment before mixing into the resin
  2. Added directly into the resin during compounding
  3. Dilute pre-treatment on the substrate surface before coating or lamination

The right dosage depends on the filler surface area, the resin system and the performance you need — typically 0.5–1.5% by weight of filler (up to 2% when added directly into resin). Because the optimum is system-specific, we recommend a small trial batch before scaling to production.
