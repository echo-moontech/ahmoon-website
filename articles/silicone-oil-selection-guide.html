How to Choose the Right Silicone Oil: A Practical Selection Guide

The single most common mistake in silicone oil selection is choosing by brand or price. The right grade is determined by viscosity, chemistry and end-use — and getting it wrong costs more than the oil saved.

📅 September 14, 2026 📂 Lubrication · Cosmetics · Coatings · Textile ⏱️ 7 min read

What Is Silicone Oil?

Silicone oil is a polymer based on polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) — a backbone of alternating silicon and oxygen atoms with two methyl groups attached to each silicon. This Si–O–Si structure gives silicone oils their characteristic properties: low surface tension, high thermal stability, excellent dielectric properties, and wide operating temperature range.

Unlike mineral oils, silicone oils are chemically inert and do not carbonize easily at high temperatures. They are supplied in a wide range of viscosities from water-thin (0.65 cSt) to extremely viscous (over 1,000,000 cSt), and in several chemical types suited to different performance requirements.

Viscosity: The Primary Selection Parameter

Viscosity is measured in centistokes (cSt) and determines almost everything about how the oil behaves in your process. The table below is a practical starting reference — use it to narrow your options before matching to a chemical type.

Viscosity Range Typical Feel / Behaviour Common Applications
0.65 – 10 cStWater-thin, highly mobileAnti-foam agents, release agents, cosmetics (quick absorb)
10 – 100 cStLight, pourableTextile lubricants, cosmetic base oils, defoamers
100 – 1,000 cStMedium body, free-flowingGeneral industrial lubrication, cosmetics, dust control

When you are uncertain of the right viscosity for your process, the most practical approach is to describe your application — process temperature, substrate, method of application and performance requirement — and let us recommend a grade. We maintain viscosity tolerances of ±5% on standard grades.

Types of Silicone Oil: A Quick Comparison

Five chemical types cover the majority of industrial and cosmetic applications. Each offers a distinct performance profile.

🔵 Dimethyl Silicone Oil (201 Series)

The workhorse type. Inert, thermally stable (-40 to 200°C), electrically insulating. Standard for industrial lubrication, release, defoaming and thermal transfer.

1 – 1,000,000 cSt

🔘 D5 Cyclic Siloxane

Volatile cyclic siloxane (evaporates at room temperature). Light texture, excellent spreadability. Widely used in cosmetics and as RTV-2 release agents.

4 – 6 cSt (volatile)

🟣 Methyl Phenyl Silicone Oil

Phenyl-modified PDMS. Retains fluidity and stability at temperatures where standard PDMS degrades. Higher refractive index. For high-temp and radiation applications.

10 – 800 cSt

⚡ Hydrogen-Containing Silicone Oil

Contains Si–H bonds. Reacts with alkenes and hydroxyl groups to form cured films. Used in waterproofing textiles, personal care conditioning, and addition-cure formulations.

5 – 300 cSt

💜 Vinyl Silicone Oil

Contains vinyl groups (-CH=CH₂) at chain ends or side chains. Essential raw material for addition-cure (platinum-catalysed) silicone rubber — LSR, HTV and RTV-2 systems.

100 – 100,000 cSt

Selection by Application

Once you have narrowed the viscosity range, the chemical type is determined by the application environment. Here are the most common use cases and the type we typically recommend.

🏭 General Industrial Lubrication

Use dimethyl silicone oil (201 series), viscosity 50–1,000 cSt depending on the mechanism. Low friction, wide temperature range, chemically inert toward most metals and polymers.

🧴 Cosmetics & Personal Care

Choose D5 for light, quick-drying textures in serums and deodorants. Low-viscosity dimethicone for smooth skin feel in lotions. Dimethiconol in D5 for durable film-forming in hair conditioners.

🧵 Textile Finishing

Hydrogen-containing silicone oil for durable softness and hydrophobicity on fabrics. Dimethyl oil for fiber lubricants in spinning and weaving. Dosage typically 0.5–5 g/L bath.

🏗️ Mold Release

D5 or low-viscosity dimethyl silicone oil (5–50 cSt) applied as a thin film before casting. D5 evaporates after use, leaving a clean part. Non-evaporating grades for reusable moulds.

🔥 High-Temperature Lubrication

Methyl phenyl silicone oil retains viscosity and does not coke at 250–300°C where standard dimethyl oil degrades. For oven chains, conveyor belts and thermal oil systems.

💧 Waterproofing & Surface Treatment

Hydrogen-containing silicone oil reacts with -OH groups on mineral surfaces (stone, concrete, glass) to form a durable hydrophobic film. Applied diluted in solvent or emulsion.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the difference between dimethyl silicone oil and functional silicone oils?
Dimethyl silicone oil (PDMS) is the most widely used type — inert, thermally stable, and suitable for general lubrication, release and defoaming. Functional variants carry reactive groups: phenyl for high-temperature and radiation resistance; hydrogen-containing for waterproofing and cure reactions; vinyl for addition-cure rubber and further modification; hydroxy for condensation-cure systems. Choosing the right type depends on whether the oil needs to react with other materials or simply provide a stable lubricating film.
Q: How do I choose the right viscosity for my application?
Viscosity is the single most important selection parameter. General rules: 5–50 cSt for sprayable or thin-film applications (anti-foam, release agents); 100–1,000 cSt for manual lubrication, cosmetics and textile finishing; 1,000–10,000 cSt for hydraulic fluids, mold release and general industrial use; 10,000–100,000 cSt for high-viscosity lubrication, sealants and polymer modification; 100,000+ cSt for thick greases and rubber compounding. When in doubt, share your process conditions and we will recommend a starting viscosity.
Q: What is the difference between D5, D6 and dimethyl silicone oil?
D5 (cyclopentasiloxane, CAS 541-02-6) and D6 (cyclomethicone, CAS 540-97-6) are cyclic siloxanes — small ring-structured molecules that evaporate readily at room temperature. They are widely used in cosmetics for their light texture and quick-drying feel. Linear dimethyl silicone oil (201 series) is a chain-structured PDMS that does not evaporate and is the standard choice for industrial lubrication, hydraulic fluids and thermal transfer applications. The key distinction is volatility: cyclic types evaporate, linear types persist.
Q: Can silicone oil be used in high-temperature environments?
Standard dimethyl silicone oil begins to gel above 250°C. For high-temperature applications (200–300°C continuous), use phenyl-modified silicone oil, which retains its properties through thermal-oxidative environments. For extreme temperatures above 300°C or where radiation resistance is needed, fluorosilicone oil or specialized high-phenyl fluids are appropriate. For cryogenic applications below -40°C, low-phenyl silicone oil maintains fluidity where standard PDMS becomes waxy.
Q: Which silicone oil is best for cosmetics and personal care?
The most common choices for cosmetics are D5 (cyclopentasiloxane) for light, quick-drying textures in antiperspirants and serums; low-viscosity dimethyl silicone oil (2–10 cSt) for silky skin feel in lotions and creams; and high-viscosity dimethiconol in D5 blends for durable film-forming in conditioners and hair serums. Methyl phenyl silicone oil provides a unique velvety sensation and higher refractive index for premium skin-care and lip products. All should comply with the relevant FDA or EU cosmetic regulations for the target market.
