Silicone Oil Technical Guide: Dimethyl (201), Hydroxyl (107) & Vinyl Series

One polymer backbone, three very different materials — how end-group chemistry decides whether silicone oil is an inert fluid or a curing base polymer, with real batch COA data for every grade we ship.

📅 Published 2026-11-02 ⏱ 10 min read 📂 Silicone Oil ✅ Verified against batch COA

Silicone oil is the workhorse of the organosilicon industry — but "silicone oil" is not one product. Depending on the end-group chemistry, the same polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) backbone becomes either an inert, non-reactive fluid or a reactive base polymer that cures into rubber and sealants. This guide walks through the three series we manufacture — 201 dimethyl, 107 hydroxyl and vinyl silicone oil — with the actual specification tables and batch COA data we ship against.

1. Dimethyl Silicone Oil (201 Series, CAS 63148-62-9)

Dimethyl silicone oil is a linear PDMS with the formula (CH3)3SiO[(CH3)2SiO]nSi(CH3)3 — every chain end is capped with an inert trimethylsiloxy group, so the molecule carries no reactive sites. That inertness is exactly what buyers want: the fluid stays stable across roughly –50 °C to +200 °C, resists oxidation, and keeps a flat viscosity–temperature curve.

Key properties confirmed on our TDS: excellent lubrication, low surface tension, good dielectric properties, high flash point and good skin-feel — which is why 201 grades appear everywhere from textile finishing to cosmetics.

2. Hydroxyl Silicone Oil (107 Series, CAS 70131-67-8)

107 silicone oil (OH polymer) keeps the same PDMS backbone but leaves the two chain ends as reactive silanol groups: HO–Si(CH3)2O–[Si(CH3)2O]n–Si(CH3)2–OH (α,ω-diol PDMS). Once viscosity exceeds about 2500 mPa·s the material is commonly called 107 silicone rubber — it is the base polymer that cures with crosslinkers into RTV-1/RTV-2 sealants, molds and gels.

Service range is –50 °C to +250 °C with excellent electrical resistivity, high and low temperature resistance, low surface tension and water repellency.

Where 107 is used

  • Sealant base polymer — the main raw material of deacetic / dealcohol RTV silicone sealants (with 3–5% crosslinker and 0.5–1% catalyst).
  • Paper anti-tack agents — normal and high viscosity grades as the release base.
  • RTV-2 molds & gels — base and diluting material for condensed room-temperature-vulcanizing rubber.
  • Structure controller — low molecular weight grades (25–30 cSt, ~8% OH) control the structure of silicone rubber compounds, replacing diphenyl silanediol and improving transparency and processability.
  • Temperature-resistant paints in power and electrical equipment.

3. Vinyl Silicone Oil (CAS 68083-19-2)

Vinyl silicone oil is vinyl-terminated PDMS: CH2=CH–Si(CH3)2O–[(CH3)2SiO]n–Si(CH3)2–CH=CH2. The Si-Vi end groups react cleanly with Si-H crosslinkers (hydrogen silicone oil) under a platinum catalyst — the addition-cure chemistry behind LSR, HTV rubber and LED potting compounds. It is also used as a reinforcing filler base to raise hardness and strength, and reacts with polyurethane or acrylic systems to yield new coating materials.

5. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the difference between 201, 107 and vinyl silicone oil?
201 dimethyl silicone oil is fully end-capped (trimethylsiloxy) PDMS — chemically inert and non-reactive, used as a fluid: lubrication, release, defoaming, finishing. 107 hydroxyl silicone oil is an α,ω-diol PDMS whose two silanol (Si-OH) end groups react with crosslinkers, making it the base polymer of RTV-1/RTV-2 sealants. Vinyl silicone oil carries Si-Vi end groups that react with Si-H crosslinkers in addition-cure systems (LSR, HTV, LED potting). In short: 201 = inert fluid, 107 = condensation-cure base, vinyl = addition-cure base.
Q: Which viscosity grade should I choose for my application?
As a rule of thumb: below 100 cSt for release coatings and mold-making dilution; 350–1000 cSt for textile and leather finishing, personal care and general lubrication; 5,000–10,000 cSt for defoamers and paper anti-tack; above 10,000 cSt (107 or vinyl) as the base polymer of sealants and gels. When in doubt, send us your application and target texture — we will recommend 2–3 grades with samples for comparison.
Q: What does vinyl content mean, and why does it decrease at higher viscosity?
Vinyl content is the molar percentage of vinyl (CH2=CH–) groups on the polymer chain ends and pendants; it determines crosslinking density and therefore the hardness/elasticity of the cured rubber. For linear vinyl-terminated oils the number of vinyl groups per gram falls as chain length grows, so a 100 cSt grade carries about 2.8–3.1 mol% while a 100,000 cSt grade carries only 0.08–0.15 mol%. Specify the crosslinker ratio against the actual vinyl value on the COA.

Q: How should silicone oil be stored, and what is the shelf life?
Store sealed in a ventilated, dry place away from strong light and heat. Shelf life is 24 months when properly stored for dimethyl grades; re-inspect before use afterwards. 107 and vinyl silicone oils are reactive materials — keep containers tightly closed and avoid contact with moisture, amines or other contaminants.
