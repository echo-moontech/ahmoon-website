Two-Component Platinum-Cure Silicone (RTV-2 & LSR): How It Works and How to Select

The A:B chemistry behind addition-cure mold rubbers and liquid silicone rubber — one-part vs two-part systems, the six specifications that decide success, and application-specific selection for molds, soft toys and electronics, with real M-E610 data.

📅 November 25, 2026 📂 RTV-2 · LSR · Mold Making · Encapsulation ⏱️ 8 min read

What "Two-Component Platinum-Catalyzed Addition-Type" Means

Strip the jargon and the phrase describes a simple idea: a silicone that cures by chemical crosslinking (addition cure), catalyzed by platinum, and ships as two parts that you mix before use. Part A and Part B are blended in a defined ratio, poured or injected, and the mix crosslinks into a solid elastomer — at room temperature for RTV-2 systems, or in seconds inside a hot mold for LSR.

The catalyst doing the work is the industry-standard Karstedt platinum catalyst, which drives hydrosilylation: Si–H groups on the crosslinker add across vinyl groups on the polymer, forming stable Si–C bonds with zero by-products. That single fact explains why platinum cure wins for precision work:

  • No cure by-products — nothing evaporates off, so shrinkage stays at or below 0.1% and thick sections cure bubble-free
  • Depth-independent cure — the reaction is chemical, not moisture-driven, so a deep pour cures through at the same rate as the surface
  • Food-contact grades possible — with no alcohol or acetic acid released, FDA food-grade formulations are standard

The contrast is condensation-cure (tin-catalyzed) RTV: cheaper, but it releases alcohol as it cures, shrinks more, and cures surface-first — a poor fit for precision molds, thick castings and skin-contact goods.

One-Part vs Two-Part Platinum Systems

Both exist, and the right choice depends on your production style:

For wearables and small electronics, single-component platinum systems remove a mixing step — and because addition cure releases no by-products, encapsulated components stay clean and stress-free. For anything with a defined pot-life requirement or a deep pour, two-component remains the tool of choice. The Six Specifications That Decide Success

When comparing two-component platinum-cure grades (RTV-2 or LSR), check these six parameters against your process — they are the ones that actually change outcomes on the floor:

  1. Mixing ratio — 1:1 by weight is the easiest to meter by hand; 10:1 ratios suit meter-mix dispensing equipment. Either way, ratio accuracy directly controls final hardness and cure completeness.
  2. Viscosity — low viscosity (around 1,800 cps) flows into fine model detail and releases air; high viscosity suits coating and brush-up work. "Adjustable" viscosity means the grade can be tuned to your process — ask.
  3. Operating time (pot life) — how long the mix stays pourable at 25 °C. You need enough window to degas, pour and level, but not so long that demold waits all day.
  4. Curing time — compare "basic" cure (practical demold) and "perfect" cure (full properties). Gentle heat accelerates both.
  5. Hardness & tear strength — Shore A hardness decides feel and flexibility (Shore A 10 for soft, flexible molds and cast parts; Shore A 20–40 for production tooling). Tear strength decides how many demold cycles the mold survives.
  6. Shrinkage — addition cure should be ≤ 0.1%. If a datasheet is vague here, ask: it is the clearest tell of a true platinum system.

Matching the Grade to the Application

🏗️ Mold Making & Casting

Art crafts, candles, gypsum ornaments, architectural elements, prototypes. Priorities: low viscosity for detail, ≤ 0.1% shrinkage for dimensional accuracy, tear strength for mold life. This is the home ground of RTV-2 grades like M-E610.

🧸 Stress-Relieving (Squishy) Toys

Soft toys need a low Shore A grade (around Shore A 10 or softer) with high elongation, platinum-safe pigments, and a skin-contact-suitable formulation — platinum cure releases no by-products, which is why it is preferred over tin-cure here. Confirm durometer target and wall thickness with your supplier before committing.

📱 3C Electronics & Wearables

Encapsulation and over-molding for wearables, sensors and small assemblies. Single-component platinum systems simplify dispensing; two-component gives pot-life control for deep sections. Addition cure's zero-by-product chemistry keeps components clean and uncorroded.

🍽️ Food-Contact Tooling

Bakeware, candy and chocolate molds, food-grade gaskets. Requires an FDA food-grade formulation — always ask for the compliance documentation with your sample, not just the datasheet claim.

One Failure Mode to Know Before You Mix

Platinum chemistry has a single well-known weakness: cure inhibition. Trace amines, organotin (the catalyst in condensation-cure RTV-1), sulfur and polysulfides, phosphorus compounds and contaminated fillers all poison the catalyst — the mix never gels or stays tacky. It is a contamination problem, not a dosing problem. Dedicated clean tooling, nitrile gloves, platinum-safe fillers with COA, and a small bench test before production will prevent essentially all cases. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is two-component platinum-cure silicone?
A two-part addition-cure system: Part A typically carries the platinum (Karstedt) catalyst, Part B the Si-H crosslinker. Mixed in a defined ratio, hydrosilylation crosslinks the vinyl-terminated silicone at room temperature with zero by-products — giving low shrinkage, depth-independent cure and food-contact grade options that condensation (tin) cure cannot match.
Q: What is the difference between RTV-2 and LSR?
Both are two-component platinum-catalyzed addition-type silicones; the difference is processing. RTV-2 is pourable and cures at room temperature in 3–12 hours — mold making, casting, encapsulation. LSR is a higher-consistency compound injection-molded in hot molds, gelling in seconds. The selection logic (ratio, viscosity, pot life, hardness, inhibition) is the same for both.
Q: Single-component or two-component platinum silicone for electronics and wearables?
Single-component systems come pre-mixed and inhibitor-stabilized: no weighing or mixing, gel on heating — convenient for 3C wearable and small encapsulation runs. Two-component gives control over pot life and cure speed for deep-section potting. Both avoid the under-cured cores of moisture-cure chemistry in thick sections; we will confirm suitability for your end use before you commit.
Q: Can platinum-cure silicone be used for stress-relieving (squishy) toys?
Yes — specify a low Shore A grade (around Shore A 10 or softer) with high elongation, platinum-safe pigments and a skin-contact-suitable formulation. Platinum cure leaves no cure by-products, which is why it is preferred over tin-cure for skin-contact goods. Tell us your durometer target and wall thickness and we will confirm the suitable grade.
Q: Why is my two-component addition-cure silicone not curing?
Almost always catalyst poisoning, not under-dosing. Amines, organotin, sulfur and polysulfides, phosphorus compounds and contaminated fillers inhibit platinum — symptoms are delayed cure or tacky surfaces. Use dedicated clean tooling, nitrile gloves, platinum-safe fillers with COA, and run a small inhibition test before production batches.
Q: Which specifications matter most when selecting?
Six: mixing ratio (1:1 easiest by hand, 10:1 suits meter-mix), viscosity (low viscosity like 1,800 cps flows into fine detail), operating time at shop temperature, curing time (basic and full), hardness and tear strength, and shrinkage (≤ 0.1% for true addition cure). Always validate pot life and demold on your own floor — both are tunable with ratio and temperature.
