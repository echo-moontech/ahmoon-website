Octyltriethoxysilane

n-Octyltriethoxysilane / Triethoxyoctylsilane

CAS: 2943-75-1

Octyltriethoxysilane is a hydrophobic silane coupling agent that forms a durable water-repellent monolayer on glass, silica, and inorganic mineral surfaces. Its ethoxy groups hydrolyze to bond with substrates while the long C8 alkyl chain lowers surface energy for strong moisture resistance. Ideal for glass hydrophobization, filler surface modification, masonry water-repellent coatings, and anticorrosion primers.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is octyltriethoxysilane used for?
Octyltriethoxysilane (CAS 2943-75-1, also called n-octyltriethoxysilane or triethoxyoctylsilane) is a hydrophobic silane used to treat the surface of glass, silica, and inorganic mineral fillers. It forms a water-repellent monolayer that reduces surface energy, improving moisture resistance, dispersibility in polymers, and anti-sticking properties. Common uses include glass hydrophobization, powder surface modification, masonry water-repellent coatings, and anticorrosion primers.

How do you use octyltriethoxysilane for hydrophobic glass treatment?
Dissolve 1-5% octyltriethoxysilane in an alcohol or hydrocarbon solvent, optionally with a small amount of water and acid catalyst (pH 4-5), and allow pre-hydrolysis for 15-30 minutes. Apply to clean glass or silica by dip, spray, or wipe, then cure at 80-120°C for 30-60 minutes to form a durable hydrophobic film. Contact angle above 100° is typically achieved.

What is the difference between octyltriethoxysilane and other alkyl silanes?
Octyltriethoxysilane carries a longer C8 alkyl chain compared with shorter-chain silanes such as methyltrimethoxysilane or ethyltriethoxysilane. The longer hydrophobic chain gives a lower surface energy and stronger water repellency, making it preferred for durable glass and mineral hydrophobization. The triethoxy group hydrolyzes to form silanol bonds with inorganic surfaces.
